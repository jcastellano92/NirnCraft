# Tamriel: the world plan

Skycraft's overworld is Nirn. You spawn in Skyrim, outside Whiterun. The nine holds and the other provinces of
Tamriel sit where they do on the real map. Past Tamriel's seas the world keeps going: Atmora, Akavir, Yokuda,
Pyandonea and Thras, then endless generated lands that reuse every biome type. Skycraft generates all of it
itself: terrain, biomes, surface, vegetation, structures and towns. No third-party terrain, structure or village
mods are used.

This file is the contract for every workstream that builds the world. If code and this file disagree, fix one
of them.

## 1. Scale and coordinates

* One block is about one metre. **Skyrim is about 6,000 blocks wide** (west to east), roughly the walkable size
  of the game. The other provinces keep their real proportions to Skyrim, so **Tamriel is about 18,000 × 16,000
  blocks**.
* **Block (0, 0) is Whiterun.** North is −Z and east is +X, matching Minecraft's compass.
* **The Tamriel map is a raster of 32-block cells.** Its bounds are x ∈ [−10240, 10240) and z ∈ [−6656, 11264),
  which is 640 × 560 cells. It includes Tamriel's coastal seas and Solstheim. Everything outside the raster is
  generated procedurally (section 3).
* The layout is fixed for every seed. The seed only changes the detail noise: hills, trees, caves and the
  placement of generic sites.

## 2. The map assets (class-path resources, used by server and client)

These live under `mod/src/main/resources/skycraft/tamriel/`. They are not datapack files: the map is needed
before datapacks load, and the client map screen needs it too.

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `regions.png` | 640×560, 8-bit indexed. Each pixel is a region index into `regions.json` (0 = open sea). |
| `elevation.png` | 640×560, 16-bit grey. Macro surface elevation in blocks, offset by +64, so 64 = sea level y 63. |
| `features.png` | 640×560 RGBA bit flags: R = river strength, G = mountain ridge strength, B = lake, A = road corridor and settlement pad (calms the terrain detail and keeps cave mouths shut). |
| `peaks.json` | The sharp peaks (the Throat of the World, Mount Anthor) that `terrain/MapPeaks` draws at full resolution on top of the raster: profile, aretes, High Hrothgar's ledge, and the graded 7,000 Steps. |
| `regions.json` | Region definitions (section 5). |
| `places.json` | Named places with block coordinates (section 6). |

* **Source of the assets.** They are generated by `tools/worldgen/tamriel_map.py` from hand-authored vector data
  in `tools/worldgen/tamriel_vectors.json`:
  * province and hold polygons;
  * coastlines and islands;
  * mountain ranges as polylines with a height and width;
  * rivers as polylines;
  * lakes as polygons;
  * named peaks.

  The vectors use block coordinates, so the script just rasterises them. **Re-run the script after editing the
  vectors**, and commit both the vectors and the PNGs.
* **Geography to get right.** The vectors must trace the real lore map:
  * Skyrim: the Throat of the World (the highest peak, y ≈ 560, east of Whiterun), the Velothi Mountains on
    the Morrowind border, the Jerall Mountains on the Cyrodiil border, the Druadach Mountains in the Reach,
    the White River, the Karth, Lake Ilinalta, Lake Honrich, the Sea of Ghosts coast and Solstheim.
  * Cyrodiil: Lake Rumare and the Imperial City island, the Niben, the Gold Coast and Topal Bay.
  * Morrowind: Vvardenfell with Red Mountain, the Inner Sea, and Mournhold.
  * Other provinces: the Wrothgarian Mountains, the Alik'r Desert, the Dragontail Mountains, the Valus
    Mountains, the Iliac Bay, the Abecean Sea, the Summerset Isles and Auridon, Valenwood's coast, and the
    Black Marsh delta.

## 3. Beyond Tamriel

`TamrielMap` also answers for any coordinate outside the raster:

* **The fixed foreign lands.** Each is a large landmass placed beyond Tamriel's seas in its lore direction and
  shaped by seeded noise:
  * **Atmora** to the north: frozen.
  * **Akavir** to the east. Its sub-regions are Tsaesci jungles, Kamal snowfields, Tang Mo islands and
    Ka Po' Tun steppe.
  * **Yokuda** to the west: a desert and a sunken archipelago.
  * **Pyandonea** to the south-west: jungle isles.
  * **Thras** to the south-west: coral islands.
* **Endless lands.** Further out, a jittered grid of 12,288-block super-cells. Each cell holds an ocean, an
  archipelago or a continent.
  * Each landmass gets a realm archetype, which is a palette of biome families: frozen, temperate, ash,
    swamp, desert, jungle, crystal, savanna, mushroom or alpine.
  * Landmasses get generated names in the "Uncharted" style, plus seeded regions inside them.
  * They reuse every biome in the catalogue, so the world never runs out.
* Regions out here are generated `Region` objects with stable ids such as `akavir/tsaesci_3` or `realm/x12_z-4/2`.
  They have the same API as authored regions.

## 4. Terrain pipeline (world preset → noise settings → density functions)

* **Overrides.**
  * `data/minecraft/worldgen/world_preset/normal.json` (and `large_biomes`) points the overworld at
    `skycraft:tamriel` noise settings and the `skycraft:tamriel` biome source. Amplified stays vanilla.
  * `data/minecraft/dimension_type/overworld.json` raises the world top: `min_y` −64, `height` 704 (top at
    y 639). The terrain noise fills up to y 575 (`noise.height` 640); above that is open sky.
* **Heights.** Sea level is y 63. Ordinary hills are y 90–180; the Rift and Falkreath are highlands at y 150–190;
  the great ranges (Jerall, Velothi, Druadach, Wrothgarian, Dragontail) reach y 300–480 with passes at y 180–250
  where the map's roads cross them; Red Mountain stands at about y 480 round its caldera; the Throat of the World
  peaks near y 560 with High Hrothgar on a ledge at about y 434. The client draws the overworld's clouds at y 330
  (`terrain/client/TamrielSkyEffects`), so the big peaks rise through them.
* **Custom density function types.** These are registered in `Registries.DENSITY_FUNCTION_TYPE`.
  * `skycraft:tamriel_channel` has a `channel` field: `continentalness`, `erosion`, `ridges`, `elevation`,
    `river` or `lake`. It samples the map with bilinear interpolation plus a small seeded domain warp, so
    borders and coasts aren't on a 32-block grid.
  * It must be **thread-safe and allocation-free**: Distant Horizons and worldgen workers call it from many
    threads.
  * Wrap it in `cache_2d` in the JSON.
* **How the noise settings combine.**
  * The map supplies the macro shape: land versus sea, mountain ranges and lowlands.
  * Vanilla-style noises supply the local detail.
  * Map continentalness and erosion feed vanilla's terrain splines, so slopes, cliffs and peaks look natural.
  * Mountain-ridge strength raises jaggedness, and the river and lake channels carve.
  * Road corridors and settlement pads (the A channel of `features.png`) calm the detail, keep cave mouths shut
    and turn mountain ranges into passes; settlements stand on ground levelled to its local mean.
  * Marshes (wetland) hover round sea level with a maze of channels that the `map_water` feature fills.
* **Surface rules** come from the biome catalogue (section 5). Each biome has a surface preset: grass, snow,
  ash, sand, red sand, mud, moss, podzol, gravel, stone, crystal or coral.
* **Caves.** Vanilla noise caves stay. Aquifers and ore veins stay on, using Skycraft ores.

## 5. Regions and biomes

### Regions (`regions.json`)

```json
{ "id": "skyrim/whiterun", "province": "skyrim", "kind": "hold", "culture": "nord",
  "name": "region.skycraft.skyrim.whiterun", "levels": [1, 12], "palette": "whiterun_tundra",
  "music": "skyrim", "weather": "skyrim_temperate", "capital": "whiterun" }
```

* **Provinces:** `skyrim`, `cyrodiil`, `morrowind`, `black_marsh`, `elsweyr`, `valenwood`, `summerset`,
  `hammerfell` and `high_rock`, plus the foreign lands `atmora`, `akavir`, `yokuda`, `pyandonea`, `thras` and
  `realm` (generated).
* **Skyrim's regions** are its nine holds plus Solstheim, which is politically Morrowind but reached from
  Skyrim: `haafingar`, `hjaalmarch`, `the_pale`, `winterhold`, `the_reach`, `whiterun`, `eastmarch`,
  `falkreath`, `the_rift`, `solstheim`.
* **Other provinces** are split the same way, into Cyrodiil's counties, Morrowind's districts, High Rock's and
  Hammerfell's kingdoms, Elsweyr's Anequina and Pellitine, and so on.
* **Region level bands** rise with distance from Whiterun along the lore's danger gradient. Leveling,
  loot and quests use `Regions.at(...).levels()`.

### Biomes (`skycraft:*`)

* **The catalogue is generated.** `tools/worldgen/biomes.py` holds a declarative table and writes:
  * `data/skycraft/worldgen/biome/*.json`;
  * the surface-rule section of `skycraft:tamriel` noise settings;
  * biome tags (Skycraft's `skycraft:spawns/*` and `skycraft:has_structure/*`, plus vanilla and Forge tags
    such as `#minecraft:is_forest` and `#forge:is_snowy`, so other code keeps working);
  * lang names.
* **Feature order.** Every biome's feature lists come from one canonical ordered feature list per generation
  step. That rules out the "feature order cycle" crash by construction. Never hand-edit a generated biome.
* **Palettes.** A palette is a small table that picks a biome within a region from local climate noise and
  terrain context: coast/beach, river, lowland, hill, peak, and cave depth.
  * Every region's palette must cover ocean, beach and river, so coasts and rivers always get a sensible
    biome.
  * Palettes live in `skycraft/tamriel/palettes.json`. Keys are region ids, `province/<province>`, `realm/<archetype>`
    and `default`; a region finds its palette by id, then by its `palette` field (a key, or an archetype name such
    as `frozen`), then by province. Missing contexts fall back along `fallback`. Contexts: `ocean`, `deep_ocean`,
    `beach`, `river`, `lake`, `marsh`, `lowland`, `upland`, `peak`, `summit`, `cave`, `deep_cave`.
  * `upland_y`, `peak_y` and `summit_y` follow each region's ground. An optional `bias` shifts the climate value by
    position so the choices follow geography: `linear` (e.g. Solstheim's pine north and ash south) or `radial`
    (only the first entries near a centre, e.g. tundra round Whiterun, aspens round Riften).
  * The biome source reads the noise router like vanilla does: `depth` (about 0 at the surface, +1 per 128 blocks
    down) for caves and the surface height, `continentalness` for sea (< −0.19), deep sea (< −0.455) and coast
    (< −0.11), `erosion` for marshes and `ridges` for valley rivers at water level, plus `temperature` and
    `vegetation` for variety inside a palette. From the map it uses `coastDistance`, `river`, `lake`, `ridge` and
    `elevation`. `/skybiome [pos]` and `/skybiome census <radius> <palette>` show what it picks.
* **Starter catalogue (about 90 biomes).** Workstreams may add more.

### New blocks for biomes

Ash and ash layers, volcanic rock, aspen leaves (yellow and orange), Morrowind mushroom stem and cap, Graht-oak
log, wood and leaves, palm log and fronds, Hist sap leaves, crystal glass and crystal growths, coral stone,
dune sand, and swamp mud reeds.

* Textures are generated with Python and PIL in `tools/textures/tamriel.py`.
* Trees use vanilla trunk and foliage placers where possible. Giant trees (Graht-oak, Telvanni mushrooms, Hist)
  are Java features that stay inside the 3×3 chunk write region.

## 6. Places (`places.json`)

```json
{ "id": "whiterun", "name": "place.skycraft.whiterun", "type": "capital", "culture": "nord",
  "region": "skyrim/whiterun", "x": 0, "z": 0, "radius": 160 }
```

* **Types:**
  * Settlements: `capital`, `city`, `town`, `village`, `farm`.
  * Fixed landmarks: `fort`, `standing_stone`, `word_wall`, `dragon_mound`, `daedric_shrine`, `landmark`.
  * `dungeon_fixed`: named barrows and ruins with lore, e.g. Bleak Falls Barrow and Labyrinthian.
* **Skyrim has all of these:**
  * The 9 hold capitals.
  * The towns: Riverwood, Rorikstead, Ivarstead, Helgen, Dragon Bridge, Karthwasten, Kynesgrove, Shor's
    Stone, Darkwater Crossing, Old Hroldan, Soljund's Sinkhole, Stonehills, Heartwood Mill, Sarethi Farm.
  * The 13 standing stones.
  * High Hrothgar and the Throat of the World.
  * The College of Winterhold, Sky Haven Temple, the Blackreach entrances, and the major named dungeons.
* **Other provinces** list their capitals and main cities: the Imperial City, Anvil, Kvatch, Skingrad, Chorrol,
  Bruma, Cheydinhal, Bravil, Leyawiin; Vivec, Mournhold, Balmora, Ald'ruhn, Sadrith Mora, Raven Rock; and so on.
* **Generic towns, villages, farms, forts and dungeons** are not listed. They come from random-spread
  structure sets filtered by region culture.

## 7. Java contracts (package `com.skycraft.tamriel`)

All methods are static, thread-safe and usable on both logical sides, unless noted.

```java
TamrielMap.sample(int x, int z, MapSample out)  // allocation-free: region index, blend partner + weight,
                                                // elevation, ridge, river, lake, coast distance
TamrielMap.region(int x, int z) -> Region       // authored or generated
TamrielMap.elevation(int x, int z) -> float     // macro surface height in blocks
TamrielMap.inTamriel(int x, int z) -> boolean

Region(String id, String province, String kind, String culture, Component name,
       int minLevel, int maxLevel, String palette, String music, String weather, @Nullable String capital)
Regions.at(Level level, BlockPos pos) -> Region  // overworld; other dimensions return a dimension region
Regions.byId(String id) -> Optional<Region>
Regions.all() -> Collection<Region>              // authored regions only

Place(String id, Component name, PlaceType type, String culture, String regionId, int x, int z, int radius)
Places.all() / Places.byId(id) / Places.nearest(PlaceType type, int x, int z, int maxDist)
Places.inChunk(ChunkPos pos) -> List<Place>
```

`core/Holds` becomes a facade:

* `holdAt` returns `Regions.at(...).id()`.
* `displayName` returns the region name.
* `NAMES` lists the Skyrim hold ids.
* `creatures/Leveling.regionLevel` uses the region's level band, plus depth and danger bonuses.

Existing callers keep compiling.

## 8. Structures

* **Fixed places.** The structure placement type `skycraft:places` makes a chunk a structure start exactly when
  `Places.inChunk` holds a place of the structure's place type. One structure type,
  `skycraft:settlement` (`culture`, `size`), builds capitals, cities, towns and villages procedurally with a
  culture kit. It follows the same paint-per-chunk design as `dungeons/world/DungeonStructure`.
* **Culture kits** are building archetypes plus block palettes:
  * The archetypes: house, longhouse, inn, smithy, general store, temple, palace or keep, barracks, stables,
    farmhouse, walls, gates and towers, market stalls, docks.
  * Order of work: `nord` first, then `imperial`, `dunmer` (Hlaalu, Redoran, Telvanni), `argonian`, `khajiit`,
    `bosmer`, `altmer`, `redguard`, `breton`, `orc` (strongholds), `akaviri`, `atmoran`, `yokudan`, `maormer`.
* **Generic sites.** Random-spread structure sets for villages, farms, forts, camps and culture dungeons check
  `TamrielMap.region(...).culture()` in `findGenerationPoint`. For example:
  * Nordic barrows: Skyrim and Atmora.
  * Ayleid ruins: Cyrodiil.
  * Velothi tombs and Daedric ruins: Morrowind.
  * Xanmeer: Black Marsh.
* **Shared rules.** Every structure obeys the existing worldgen rules: only noise heights in
  `findGenerationPoint`, and painting clipped to the chunk.
* **Building grammar and zoning** (`tamriel/settlement/grammar`). Ordinary buildings are derived, not drawn by hand:
  a `Blueprint` (footprint rect/L/T/U, storeys, roof, foundation, porch, annex, balcony, outside stairs, materials)
  comes from the culture style sheet `skycraft/tamriel/styles/<culture>.json`, the building's `Use` and its
  `Wealth`, deterministically from its spec. `Zoning` lays out every generic settlement (and fills round the unique
  capitals): a seeded form per culture and site, tensor-field lanes, quarters, compact street frontage, squares,
  street furniture, organic or partial walls only where warranted, farms and works outside. Contracts for other
  workstreams:
  * `SettlementAnchors.gates(Place)`: world `{x, z}` road ends of a named settlement (main gate first), predicted
    from the map alone; internal streets end there. `SettlementAnchors.recordedGates(level, key)` gives the built
    ones (`AnchorType.GATE`), generic settlements included.
  * `SettlementPlan.reserve(role, size[, quarter, where])`: a lot in every matching settlement, built as a hall in
    the local style unless a `SettlementPlan.painter`/`furnisher` is registered for the role.
  * Jails and guild halls (`plan/Halls`) and player-home plots (`plan/HousePlots`) get their lots before the
    houses are packed in.
  * `/skysettle` (operators): galleries, plan/top/isometric images and density/variety metrics in
    `skycraft-previews/`.

## 10. Order of work

1. **Phase 2: Foundation.** The map tool and assets, the density functions and noise settings, the biome
   catalogue, palettes and biome source, the regions, places and Holds facade, and the map screen. Then
   remove Terralith and Tectonic.
2. **Phase 3: Skyrim content.**
   * Nord settlements and capitals, region-based houses and carriages, the standing stones, dragon mounds and
     word walls.
   * Skycraft NPC townsfolk, which replace the third-party villager mod (done: society module).
   * The ambient-sound engine and boss music, the creature roster, animations, UI and visuals.
   * Remove the remaining structure and village mods.
3. **Phase 4: Tamriel and beyond.** Each province's culture kit, dungeons, creatures and NPC races; every race
   playable; the magic and skill expansions; ships and ports for sea travel; and the foreign and generated
   lands.
